How forecasts work

No black box. Just sums you can check.

This page shows exactly how Reorder Desk decides what to reorder and how many. The same steps run for every item, and the app shows you each item's own numbers in its "Why?" panel.

The whole thing in one line

Order = (lead time + days to cover) × sales per day
+ safety stock
− in stock − already on order
then round up to whole case packs and the minimum order

In words: buy enough to last until the delivery arrives, plus the period you want each order to cover, plus a cushion for surprises, minus what you already have or have ordered. The rest of this page explains where each number comes from.

1. Sales per day

Reorder Desk adds up each item's sales per week from your Shopify orders (online and point of sale; cancelled and test orders left out). It then takes a weighted average of the weekly sales rates over the history you choose, 90 days by default:

  • Recent weeks count more. Each week back counts 0.85 times the week after it, so last week counts fully, four weeks ago about 60%, and three months ago less than 15%. Forecasts follow a trend without jumping on one odd week.
  • Out-of-stock days are left out. If an item sold 20 units in a week but was only in stock for 4 days, its rate that week is 5 a day, not 2.9. Weeks with fewer than 2 days in stock are skipped. A stock-out never lowers the forecast for the next order.
  • Bundle sales count for their parts. If a "Tea Set" bundle contains two mugs, each Tea Set sold counts as two mugs sold.

With less than four weeks of history, or fewer than five sales, the item is marked as a rough guide. Items with no sales in the history period are listed under "No recent sales" and not reordered.

2. Seasonal adjustment (Pro)

With a year of history, Reorder Desk compares last year's sales in the weeks ahead (the lead time plus the days to cover) with last year's sales in the weeks before. If sales rose by 40% at this time last year, this year's rate is raised by 40% too. Changes under 15% are ignored, the change is limited to between half and three times the normal rate, and it needs at least 10 units sold last year. The "why" shows the factor used.

3. Stock position

Stock available at the locations you count, plus stock on sent purchase orders not yet received. You can choose to count draft purchase orders and Shopify transfers in transit as well.

4. Reorder point and status

The reorder point is the stock you need to get through the lead time with the safety stock untouched: sales per day × lead time + safety stock. The status follows from it:

Each item also gets an "order by" date: the last day you can order and still have the safety stock intact when the delivery arrives.

5. Quantity and rounding

The quantity from the one-line formula is rounded up to whole case packs, then raised to the minimum order quantity if it is below it (still in whole packs). On a purchase order, Reorder Desk also warns when the order's total value is under the supplier's minimum order value.

The settings, and where to change them

Every store starts with the defaults below; change them for the whole store in Settings. Lead time can also be set per supplier, and an item's own settings in its "Why?" panel always win over the store's.

You can also override the result itself: set an item's sales per day (for a product you know is about to take off), or type the quantity to order. The "why" then says the number is yours.

Questions about the maths

Why not use AI or machine learning?

Most small and medium stores don't have enough sales per item for a complex model to beat a well-weighted average, and a number nobody can explain is hard to trust with real money. A clear method you can check and correct works better in practice.

What about a product I'm about to promote?

Set its sales per day yourself in the "Why?" panel, or type the quantity you want. Your number is used until you clear it.

A new product has no history. What happens?

Until it sells, it shows under "No recent sales" and is not reordered. Once it has a few sales, it gets a forecast marked as a rough guide until there are four weeks of history.

Does it count returns and cancellations?

Cancelled and test orders are left out. Sales of the last 35 days are read again at every sync, so later refunds and cancellations are picked up.

How often are the numbers updated?

Stock levels update live as they change in Shopify. Sales and products are synced every six hours, and you can sync any time with "Sync now".
